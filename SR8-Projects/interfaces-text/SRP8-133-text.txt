2.3.1.1 Internal Interfaces 

SRP8-124 Hydrogen Energy System: 

Involves multiple components, ensuring the longevity and efficiency of the fuel cell. Involves hydrogen pressure regulator, placed at the anode hydrogen feed stream to stabilise pressure to the desired pressure. Hydrogen feed from storage pressure vessels will be highly pressured and regulation is necessary to keep a steady driving force across the membrane while preventing over-pressurisation damage to the fuel cell.  Furthermore, this system maintains prevent back diffusion of oxygen, nitrogen and water through the membrane through a continuous flow of hydrogen throughout the system. 

The hydrogen energy system regulates the reactants present at the anode. Thus, it also consists of a purge valve, placed at the anode outlet prevents back-diffused contaminant build-up by purging periodically.  Finally, to regulate temperature, it contains a thermal management system, a closed-loop cooling system integrated with the fuel cell to carry away heat generated by the electrochemical reaction. The coolant will be deionised water. 

SRP8-131 Hydrogen Pressure Vessel: 

Stores highly pressurised hydrogen used to power the PEMFC. The hydrogen from storage will be stepped down via a pressure regulator to meet operating pressure requirements for the fuel cell.  

SRP8-179 Air Flow and Venting: 

Integration of proper airflow to introduce oxygen into the cathode of the fuel cell; thereby giving the proper reactant needed to undergo reduction to form water. The integration of this system is necessary to remove waste products and help maintain optimal temperatures of the PEMFC. 

SRP8-182 Hydrogen Electrical System: 

DC-DC converters take the variable voltage output (typically 65–100V) from the fuel cell and adjusts it to meet the 800V requirement of the battery and ECS. These converters act as voltage regulators, ensuring the electrical output is stable, reliable, and within the voltage range suitable for battery charging and load supply.
