The ceiling system interfaces with several vehicle subsystems and acts as the primary interior roof assembly within Sunswift 8. The headliner interfaces directly with the occupant cell through dedicated front and rear mounting locations. It is designed to cover the interior roof region, conceal selected electrical routing and provide an interior surface above the occupant head region.  

The ceiling console interfaces independently with the occupant cell and is not structurally supported by the headliner. The ceiling console provides the packaging and mounting provisions for the high voltage cut off as well as the rear vision system while maintaining accessibility for the driver and serviceability for the team. A channel is included in the console design to allow wiring to reach the rear vision display, which is mounted directly in for while the interior spotlights are positioned to illuminate the driver and passenger seating areas.  

The sun visor assemblies interface directly with the forward region of the ceiling structure. The mounting system was designed to integrate with the existing ceiling packaging without creating exposed protrusions or reducing occupant space. The sun visor mount has a thickness of less than 8mm, allowing it to be concealed beneath the surface of the headliner meaning that there is no possibility of edge contact with the occupant. 

The sun visor mounts are considered roof-mounted components under ADR 21 because they are attached to the ceiling but are not part of the roof structure. Therefore, exposed mount edges have been filleted to achieve a minimum radius of 3.2 mm. The projecting features will also be checked to ensure that their width is not less than their downward projection from the roof lining, or otherwise assessed against the relevant energy-dissipation requirement. 

The roof solar subsystem may interface with the ceiling through junction boxes positioned toward the rear bulkhead. The headliner is designed to cover these regions while maintaining consideration for future access and routing requirements. This routing remains subject to final vehicle packaging, as the solar wiring may alternatively be routed through the boot rather than the interior cabin.
